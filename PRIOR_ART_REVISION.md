## Closest works and explicit overlap

1. **J. Večeř**, *Frieze's zeta(3) and the Avram--Bertsimas constant under Haar conjugation*, arXiv:2611.31583, 23 Nov 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31583. Full textual version inspected. Assumption (1) imposes a dimension-uniform bound on centered eigenvalues standardized by their empirical standard deviation. Theorem 4 already proves the reciprocal q=1 L1 leading limit in that regime; Theorem 2 and Corollary 3 give a stronger mean expansion, with first inverse-log coefficient 1 − gamma/2 − pi²/12. Section 8 treats the critical polynomial spectrum and the constant 10/3. These are not new here. Section 3 uses the recursive tree (each vertex chooses the cheapest earlier edge), Lemma 6 supplies conditional sphere factorization, and Lemma 7 equation (19) supplies the inverse-row-radius bound for a<1, without the bounded-spectrum hypothesis. Lemma 8 contains exponential-maximum inverse-moment asymptotics. The section's upper bound also gives the spectrum-independent logarithmic improvement without assumption (1). Appendix A analyzes the recursive comparison. Revision 2 explicitly attributes these facts, and uses them in its proof rather than presenting them as discoveries. Its sharp uniform error is measured relative to a spectrum-dependent inverse-moment target, not a claim to improve Večeř's exact bounded-case coefficient. No identical all-spectrum mass-partition limit or exact multiplicity-governed inverse-cost moment threshold was identified in the inspected text.
2. **T. Chambrion and C. Mendico**, arXiv:2611.12502v2, https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12502; arXiv:2611.14091v1, https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14091. The motivating control and numerical papers give the control-time bound, critical conjectures, universal path upper estimate (Theorem 11, Remark 3), projected-variance identity, conditional spherical representation and single-coupling spectral-shape analysis. These are prior work. Single-coupling convergence does not by itself establish all-spectrum MST convergence or the full cut-tail exponent. The criterion here is for the MST-based upper bound, not necessary-and-sufficient control time. Textual versions were inspected; direct PDF rendering failed.
3. **Institute for Recursive Self Improvement**, *Critical Haar MST weight tends to zero* (12 Nov 2026), https://irsi.ai/notes/haar-mst-critical/. AI-developed note reports qualitative critical decay using Gaussian block approximation and inverse-moment estimates. Linked full proof not retrieved. Do not claim priority for that critical case.
4. **A. M. Frieze**, *On the value of a random minimum spanning tree problem*, Discrete Applied Mathematics 10 (1985), 47–56, https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(85)90058-7. Independent identically distributed edge costs; asymptotic zeta(3) constant under density and moment hypotheses. **F. Avram and D. Bertsimas**, *The minimum spanning tree constant in geometrical probability and under the independent model: a unified approach*, Annals of Applied Probability 2 (1992), 113–130, https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/4f79a403-6acd-4d44-b263-f050e6353eb1 (working-paper version). Neither independent-edge or geometric cost model directly supplies this inverse-power Haar-dependent result. Classical MST and dependence-robust deterministic graph arguments are not new.
5. **E. S. Meckes**, *The Random Matrix Theory of the Classical Compact Groups*, Cambridge University Press, 2019. Classical Haar sphere and frame representations underpin the proof. Normalized Gaussian/Dirichlet identities, moment formulas and exponential maxima are standard. Weighted single-coupling limits and product-weight graph results are closely related tools; their existence does not imply the tree theorem.
6. **T. Bendokat, R. Zimmermann and P.-A. Absil**, *A Grassmann manifold handbook: basic geometry and computational aspects*, Advances in Computational Mathematics (2024), https://doi.org/10.1007/s10444-023-10090-8. Standard projection and graph charts, Grassmann dimension, invariant metrics. Tube-volume scaling for compact embedded manifolds, elementary spectral rounding, complete multipartite minimum-edge-cut arguments, and stable-law domain-of-attraction arguments are classical mathematics. Revision 2 applies these to a cut event in the Haar conjugation model. The claimed result is the exact exponent and moment threshold, not the component techniques.

## Outstanding uncertainty

Full-text access was not universal. No similarity percentage, independent peer review, citation priority certification or external academic grade is supplied. Targeted searches cannot exclude unpublished results or differently phrased equivalents. In particular, the real-field fixed-dimensional theorem is not a real-field high-dimensional limit theorem; finite-dimensional constants can diverge when eigenvalues approach each other. The general-spectrum first-correction coefficient and arbitrary-spectrum q>=2 large-n regime remain open.
